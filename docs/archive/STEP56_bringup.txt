=======================================================================
STEPS 5 AND 6 - THE MULTIPLEXERS GO IN
EEG head phantom impedance meter, Rev B          23 October 2026
=======================================================================

This is the first time the measurement path is complete end to end. Up to now
you have proved the display, the touch panel, the bus and all seven I2C
devices. Nothing has actually measured anything yet.

READ THIS FIRST - A STALE NUMBER IN THE OLD GUIDE

  The bring-up step used to say the mid-scale calibration check should land
  within 50 ohms of 4990. That is WRONG for the board you built.

  R21 was changed to a 4.70 kohm part when 4.99k turned out not to be stocked
  in the Yageo MFP-25BRD52 series. The BOM has said so since September and the
  firmware expects 4700. A correct board reads about 4700, which the old text
  would have called a 290 ohm failure - sending you hunting for a fault that
  does not exist, at the worst possible moment. The guide is now corrected.

  EXPECT ABOUT 4700.


BEFORE YOU OPEN THE BAG

  1. Power down completely. Both charger ports, not just one.

  2. Ground yourself. These are CMOS analog switches. Touch something earthed
     before you touch the pins. This is the one irreversible step in the whole
     build - a damaged mux is a desolder job if the socket is not machined-pin,
     and a confusing intermittent fault if the damage is partial.

  3. Six CD74HC4067E, all identical, 24-pin DIP. Nothing distinguishes them, so
     there is nothing to mix up between U1 and U6 - but there is everything to
     mix up about WHICH WAY ROUND.


INSERTING THEM

  4. Notch to notch, every time. The silkscreen pin-1 mark and the chip notch
     point the same way. All six face the same direction on this board; if one
     of yours wants to go in the other way, stop and re-read the silkscreen
     rather than forcing it.

  5. Straighten the leads first. DIP legs splay outward from the factory and
     will not drop into a machined-pin socket. Press each row gently against a
     flat surface until the legs are square, then start all 24 pins together
     and press straight down with even thumb pressure.

  6. After each chip, look along the row at eye level. A folded-under pin is
     the classic failure here and it is nearly invisible from above. You are
     looking for 24 legs in 24 holes, none bent outward, none tucked under the
     body.

  7. U7 and U8 (SN74HC4066N, 14-pin) go in at the same step if they are not
     already fitted.


SET ONE THING IN THE FIRMWARE

  8. In phantom_meter_v2.ino, set

         #define SALT_BRIDGE 0

     Turn it back on after step 6. Salt-bridge scanning adds 31 measurements
     per cycle, which is pure latency to sit through while you are chasing
     something else. It also means a wiring fault shows up as bridge alarms on
     top of whatever you were actually looking at.


STEP 5 - SELF-CALIBRATION

  9. Power up on the charger, not the computer.

  10. The screen now shows a SELF-CALIBRATION report before the head map. This
      is new: the numbers used to go only to the serial monitor, and a failed
      calibration drew nothing at all - the map came up with 22 OPEN sites,
      which looks exactly like a board with no multiplexers in it.

      Reading it:

        1.00 k standard   fit anchor     ~1000      always right, by definition
        4.70 k check      expect ~4700   THE NUMBER THAT MATTERS
        100 k standard    fit anchor   ~100000      always right, by definition
        short channel     return path    tens of ohms
        open channel      must be open   OPEN

      The two anchors cannot be wrong: the fit is forced through them. R21 is
      the only reading that is free to disagree, which is exactly why it is the
      one worth looking at. It is coloured green within 150 ohms of 4700, amber
      to 400, red beyond.

      The report clears on a touch, on the pedal, or after eight seconds.

  11. If it says FAIL, the screen names which standard did not answer:

        "1k standard open"      R20, or the drive/sense mux pair
        "4.7k standard open"    R21
        "100k standard open"    R22
        "open channel is not open - a mux is stuck on"
                                a mux output is shorted, or an enable line is
                                stuck - suspect a bent pin on U1-U6 first
        "100k reads below 1k"   R20 and R22 swapped in their positions

      A bent-under pin on one of the six is far and away the most likely cause
      of any of these. Power down and look along the rows again before
      reaching for a meter.

  12. The head map appears with every site OPEN. That is correct - nothing is
      plugged into J1 or J2 yet.


STEP 6 - THE FIRST REAL MEASUREMENT

  13. Make a test lead that bridges J1 to J2. This is the part the old
      instructions got wrong, so it is worth understanding why.

      Nothing on the board connects the phantom connector (J1) to the cap
      connector (J2) - checked against the board file. Every reading drives
      current OUT through J1 and expects it BACK through J2; in use, the
      phantom contact, the gel and the cap electrode close that loop. The old
      test put a resistor inside one plug in J1 and a jumper inside a separate
      plug in J2. Neither touches the other, so no current could flow and every
      site would have read OPEN on a perfectly good board.

      What you need: two DB25 MALE solder-cup plugs (J1 and J2 are female) and
      a 4.7 kohm resistor. Solder the resistor, on short wires, from pin 1 of
      plug A to pin 1 of plug B. Plug A goes in J1, plug B in J2. Pin 1 is Fp1
      on both connectors.

      Optional, and worth it if you have the parts - each extra lead tests one
      more site and one more colour on the map:
          pin 14 to pin 14 through 1 kohm     Fp2 should read ~1k    green
          pin 22 to pin 22 through 6.8 kohm   Cz  should read ~6.8k  amber
          pin 10 to pin 10 through 22 kohm    Fz  should read ~22k   red
      Always the SAME pin number on both plugs: a lead from pin N to pin N is
      one site. Pins 12, 13 and 25 are not connected on either connector.

  14. Fp1 should read about 4.7 kohm and show green (4.7k is just inside the
      5k pass mark). Every site without a lead stays OPEN and grey.

      If Fp1 reads OPEN, check the lead with a meter first - pin 1 to pin 1,
      plug to plug, should read 4.7k. If the lead is good, the fault is in
      Fp1's drive or return mux channel, and pm_analog_probe's Cz screen can
      be pointed at it.

  15. Tap Fp1 and confirm the focus panel follows and shows the same value in
      large type. Then tap the header and check the mode changes ONCE per tap
      - that was fixed on 24 Oct and this is the first chance to see it.

  16. SITE ZERO (added 27 Oct 2026). On the bench the 4.72 kohm lead on Fp1
      read 5.2 k while the on-board 4.7 k standard read 4,708. The standards
      all return through U6; sites 0-15 return through U5, and the two chips'
      switch resistance differs by a few hundred ohms at 3.3 V. The firmware
      now has a per-site zero for this:

        a. Get a STRAIGHT-THROUGH DB25 male-to-male cable, all 25 pins wired
           (pin N to pin N). Not a null-modem cable, not a printer cable.
        b. Plug it between J1 and J2, power on, and on the calibration PASS
           screen tap ZERO SITES, then MEASURE. It reads all 22 paths in about
           six seconds and lists them; expect values within a few hundred ohm
           of zero, some negative. Any site showing OPEN means that pin of the
           cable is not wired - check the cable, then RETRY.
        c. Tap SAVE. The values are kept across power cycles and subtracted
           from every Mode A reading from then on. The PASS screen reports
           "Site zero stored".
        d. Put the 4.7 k lead back on pin 1. Fp1 should now read about 4.7 k.

      Repeat the zero only if U1-U6, U8 or a DB25 jack is ever changed.


WHAT TO WATCH FOR, NOW THAT READINGS ARE REAL

  THE LED CAVEAT, finally testable. When the indicator LEDs were fixed to hold
  their state rather than blink, LED current began flowing DURING a measurement
  instead of being switched off, through the same expander that drives the
  SW1-SW6 analog switches. At 2.5 uA test current this should not matter.

  Step 5 is the first time you can check. Tap the title bar to switch between
  Mode A and Mode B and watch whether a site's reading shifts with the mode. If
  it does, that is the first thing to suspect, and it is a one-line revert.

  THE MID-SCALE CHECK IS YOUR LINEARITY TELL. If R21 reads well off 4700 while
  both anchors are perfect, the chain is not linear between them. Suspect R21
  itself first, then the mux channel carrying it.

  AFTER STEP 6: set SALT_BRIDGE back to 1, and the thresholds (50 kohm trip,
  80 kohm clear) become tunable against a real gel bridge for the first time.
  Those two numbers were guesses until now.
