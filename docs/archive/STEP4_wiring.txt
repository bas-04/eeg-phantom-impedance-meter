=======================================================================
STEP 4 - I2C SCAN WITH THE CARRIER BOARD ATTACHED
Elecrow CrowPanel ESP32 HMI 7.0 inch  +  phantom meter Rev B carrier
16 October 2026
=======================================================================

GOAL

Prove that the display and all five carrier-board I2C devices share one
working bus. Nothing is measured yet and no multiplexers are needed. You are
looking for seven addresses on the screen.


WHICH CONNECTOR

Use the connector labelled I2C. Not GPIO_D, not UART0.

This is not a preference, it is the only choice. The carrier's bus is wired to
GPIO19 and GPIO20, and on this board those two pins come out on exactly one
connector:

    I2C      1020_SCL, 1019_SDA, 3V3, GND     <-- GPIO20 and GPIO19. Use this.
    GPIO_D   1038, NC, 3V3, GND               <-- GPIO38 only. Not the bus.
    UART0    1044_RX0, 1043_TX0, 3V3, GND     <-- GPIO44/43. Not the bus.

GPIO_D and UART0 stay empty for this step. They matter later, when the DRDY
line gets a pin, and not before.

Go by the silkscreen labels on the connector, not by which end you think is
pin 1. The labels are printed next to the pins for exactly this reason, and
the two signal wires are not interchangeable - swapping SDA and SCL gives a
completely dead bus with no other symptom.

The mating cable is whichever 4-pin pigtail came in the box with the board.
If it has gone missing, measure the connector pitch before ordering, since
Elecrow uses more than one size across their panels.


THE WIRING - FOUR WIRES, AND ONE YOU MUST NOT CONNECT

  FROM                          TO                       NET
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  I2C connector  1019_SDA       J3 pin 3                 SDA
  I2C connector  1020_SCL       J3 pin 4                 SCL
  I2C connector  GND            J5 pin 2  (see below)    GND
  5V pigtail     red  / +5V     J3 pin 1                 +5V
  5V pigtail     black / GND    J3 pin 2                 GND

  I2C connector  3V3            NOTHING. Cut it back and insulate it.

J3 pin 5 (DRDY) and J3 pin 6 (no connection on the board) stay empty.


*** THE ONE WAY TO DESTROY THE DISPLAY ***

Do not let the I2C connector's 3V3 wire touch J3 pin 1.

J3 pin 1 is the carrier's +5V input. There is no blocking diode anywhere
between it and the charger. If the display's 3V3 rail is tied to that pin
while the 5V pigtail is also plugged in, the 5V supply back-feeds down the
wire and drags the display's entire 3.3V rail - which powers the ESP32-S3
itself - up toward 5V. The ESP32-S3 is a 3.6V-maximum part. That is an
instant, permanent kill, and it is a plausible mistake because the two pins
sit one connector apart and both say "power".

So do not merely leave that wire unplugged. Cut it short and put heatshrink or
tape over the end, so a loose strand cannot find pin 1 while you are moving
the boards around. One snip removes the whole failure mode.


WHY NOT JUST POWER THE CARRIER FROM THAT 3V3 PIN

It is tempting - one connector, no second cable - and it does not work well:

  The carrier's 5V input goes through R14, a 10 ohm filter resistor, into U13,
  an MCP1700 3.3V LDO. Every active part on the board runs from U13's output.
  Feeding 3.3V into that chain instead of 5V gives roughly 3.3 - 0.3 (R14 and
  the LDO's own dropout) = about 2.9V on the analog rail. The MCP4725 DAC's
  minimum supply is 2.7V. You would be running the DAC 200 mV above its floor,
  and if the display's 3V3 rail sags under panel and backlight load, less than
  that. Nothing fails cleanly at that margin - you get a DAC that works on the
  bench and misbehaves during a class.

  The display's own 3.3V regulator would also have to carry the carrier's
  current on top of the panel and backlight.

If you ever do need single-cable operation, the real fix is to bridge across
U13 and feed the carrier's 3.3V rail directly, which is listed as option 3 in
the README's power section. That is a deliberate modification, not a wiring
shortcut, and it is not what step 4 is for.


WHY THE GROUND WIRE GOES TO J5 PIN 2

J3 pin 2 is the ground you are already using for the 5V pigtail, and one
header pin takes one jumper socket. Rather than twisting two wires into one
crimp, land the I2C cable's ground on J5 pin 2, which is also GND. J5 is the
2-pin footswitch header: pin 1 is FOOTSW, pin 2 is GND. J4 pin 2 is another
GND if J5 is more convenient to reach. Either gives the I2C signals a short,
direct return path right next to where they enter the board.

Both charger ports share ground internally, so there is already a DC ground
path without this wire. The wire is about the return loop, not continuity: the
path through two USB cables and the charger's internals is long, and a long
return is how a bus that scans fine at 100 kHz turns flaky at 400 kHz. Add it
now rather than chasing it later.


POWER

One two-port 5V charger. USB-C from one port to the display as usual, and a
USB-A-to-bare-wire pigtail from the other to J3 pins 1 and 2.

Both rails then come up together, which is what you want: a carrier sitting
unpowered with a live bus on its pins means current flowing into chip inputs
through their protection diodes.

Do NOT power the display from your computer's USB port. GPIO19/20 are the
ESP32-S3's native USB D-/D+ pins. A USB host actively drives them, and the
bus dies. Upload the sketch from the computer, then unplug and move the
display to the charger before looking at the result. This is the same trap as
on the CYD.

Do not use the BAT connector as a 5V source. It is a single-cell LiPo input
wired to the onboard charger, it sits anywhere from 3.0V to 4.2V depending on
state of charge, and back-feeding it means fighting a charger IC.

CHECK THE PIGTAIL BEFORE YOU TRUST IT. Plug it into the charger with the bare
ends apart and nothing else connected, and measure. You are looking for about
5V, and you want to know which lead is positive from the meter, not from the
wire colour - cheap pigtails are not reliably colour-coded. The green and
white data wires, if present, get cut back and insulated like the 3V3 wire.


PRE-FLIGHT, IN THIS ORDER

  1. Pigtail only, into the charger, nothing attached. Measure about 5V and
     confirm which lead is positive.

  2. Pigtail to J3 pins 1 and 2, display not connected at all. Power up.
     Measure U13's output: you want 3.3V, give or take a few hundredths.
     Nothing should be warm. If the rail is low or U13 heats up, stop - you
     have a short on the 3.3V rail, and finding it now is far easier than
     finding it after the display is in the loop.

  3. Power down. Connect the three I2C-connector wires. Check once more that
     the 3V3 wire is cut and insulated.

  4. Power up both ports and run the sketch.


ARDUINO IDE SETTINGS

Compile pm_i2c_scan, with board_config.h in that same folder set to

    #define PM_BOARD 2

which is how the bundle already ships. Board settings:

    Board            ESP32S3 Dev Module
    USB CDC On Boot  Disabled            <- compile-time checked
    Flash Size       4MB (32Mb)
    Partition        Huge APP (3MB No OTA/1MB SPIFFS)
    PSRAM            OPI PSRAM           <- compile-time checked

board_config.h turns the two that cost hours into hard compile errors, so if
it builds, those two are right. PSRAM in particular fails silently: without it
the 768 kB framebuffer cannot be allocated and the screen simply stays black
with no error anywhere.


WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

Seven addresses, listed down the left of the screen, refreshing every three
seconds:

    0x18    PCA9557 panel-reset expander     display
    0x20    MCP23017  U11                    carrier
    0x21    MCP23017  U12                    carrier
    0x48    ADS1115   M2                     carrier
    0x49    ADS1115   M3                     carrier
    0x5D    GT911 touch controller           display
    0x62    MCP4725   M1                     carrier

0x18 and 0x5D are the display's own and were already there in step 2. The five
new ones are the carrier. Order on screen is numerical, so it reads top to
bottom exactly as above.


IF IT DOES NOT

  Only 0x18 and 0x5D
      The bus is not reaching the carrier. Either the carrier has no power, or
      SDA and SCL are swapped, or a wire is in the wrong J3 pin. Check the 3.3V
      rail at U13 first, since that is one measurement and rules out half of
      it. Then confirm 1019_SDA goes to J3 pin 3 and 1020_SCL to J3 pin 4 -
      reversed is the single most common cause and looks identical to no
      connection at all.

  Nothing at all, screen says NONE
      Both ends are down, which usually means the bus is being held low or a
      ground is missing. Check that the I2C ground wire is actually seated on
      J5 pin 2. Also make sure the display is on the charger and not on your
      computer.

  Carrier devices appear, 0x5D disappears
      A USB host is loading GPIO19/20, or the display's 3V3 and the carrier's
      5V have met somewhere. Power down immediately and check that 3V3 wire.

  0x62 missing, everything else present
      The MCP4725 adapter board. Measure 3.3V on its VIN pad - if that is
      absent, it is a solder joint on the adapter, not a bus problem.

  0x20 or 0x21 missing
      That MCP23017 is seated backwards or not fully home in its socket.

  0x48 or 0x49 missing
      That ADS1115 module is backwards in its header, or its ADDR strap is
      not set for the address you expect.

  Addresses appear and disappear between refreshes
      Marginal pull-up or too much wire. R15 and R16 on the carrier are 4.7k
      I2C pull-ups deliberately left unpopulated, because the three breakout
      modules carry their own and the five devices answered on the CYD without
      them. Flaky is exactly the symptom they exist for - fit them. Shorten
      the I2C wires first, though; under 15 cm if you can.


AFTER THIS WORKS

Step 4 is the last step that needs no multiplexers. Steps 5 and 6 - the
self-calibration against the on-board standards, then a real impedance
reading - wait on U1 through U6. The DRDY pin decision and the port of
phantom_meter_v2 to board_config.h can both be settled in the meantime; see
STILL OPEN in the README.
